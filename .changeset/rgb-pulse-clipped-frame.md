`fixes.rgbPulse` (off by default): a frame with R = G = B in a colour feed, such as one clipped white (255, 255, 255), gives the projection's neutral 0 and stays out of POS and the rate clock. It used to pass its own level into the processor's window, about a hundred grey levels beside a pulse of about 0.01, and that one frame stayed in the 45 s window. On the synthetic face of the rgbPulse tests (30 frames a second, read once a second, seconds 16 to 40, the processor's own estimators with no engine rate), one clipped frame at 10 s left 50, 72 and 120 with no rate shown in any of the 24 seconds, and five clipped frames read 60 for a 72 (0 of 24 right) and 60 for a 50 (2 of 24); with the rule every one of those reads within 5 in 24 of 24, as with no clipped frame. With the WASM engine, one clipped frame at 10 s left 50 and 72 right in 0 of 24 seconds and 120 in 5; with the rule, 24 of 24. A clipped frame before the first colour sample since the last reset is still taken for a grey feed and passes its level: one white first frame left 50, 72 and 120 with no rate shown, with or without the rule. On 95 recorded captures fed straight to the processor with the switch on, only those holding an R = G = B sample moved: right seconds against a reference went from 1,655 to 1,675 fed the forehead region and from 2,100 to 2,096 fed the mean of the three regions. A feed that has been R = G = B since the last reset (a signal already extracted, or a grey camera) still passes its values through. The cost: a colour feed that turns grey for good gives 0 from then on, so it shows no rate once its window no longer holds colour, where the old rule passed the grey values through. With the switch off nothing changes.
