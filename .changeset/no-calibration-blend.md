New switch `fixes.noCalibrationBlend`, off by default. When the processor's own checks pass, `bpm` (and `fused_bpm` without a Muse) has been the camera calibration's prediction from the spectral and autocorrelation rates, a model never trained against another sensor: untrained it is their plain mean, and it then learns toward the processor's resolved rate by a small step per read. So when those two rates disagree, `bpm` is a rate neither measured, for 10 s or more: with an engine that reports 120 and a clean 120 pulse, the autocorrelation reads 60 (its doubling-lag test compares confidences clamped at 1, so it halves every clean rate over 110), and `bpm` reads 95.7, 104.1, 109.5, 113.1 and on to 119.7 at one read a second. With the switch on, `bpm` is the resolved rate (`resolved_bpm`), the one the resolver and the rate tracker chose from the engine's, spectral, autocorrelation and peak rates: 120 from the first read. `calibrated_bpm` still reports the prediction, the calibration still trains, and every refusal of the processor's gate is unchanged. With the pulse check on, the default for a session, the rate shown is the check's own and does not change.

Measured with the build's real WASM core in Node. On 50 recorded captures with a reference rate, each one's three face regions fed as the three face regions of this build's session path with the pulse check off, right seconds go from 2,094 to 2,047 of 6,679 (24 captures better, 24 worse: no measurable difference); through Elata main's session path they go from 1,296 to 1,625 (35 better, 7 worse), because main's spectral and autocorrelation rates disagree far more often. It stays off by default by the rule written before the runs (no more than 1% of right seconds lost): it loses 470 right seconds here and 92 on main, along with the ones it gains. The autocorrelation's halving itself is not changed: on the same captures, all resting rates, a repaired halving test changed this build's right seconds by 3 of 6,679 (2 gained, 1 lost), and on synthetic pulses it exposed the autocorrelation's whole-sample lag grid at 15 and 20 frames a second.
