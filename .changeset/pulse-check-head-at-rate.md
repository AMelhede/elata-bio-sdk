Rule `headMotion` (`pulseCheckRules.headMotion`, on by default) changes what it decides. It used to take the head's strongest movement line and count every other rhythm of the head as noise, so a smaller second rhythm (a sway beside a nod) could hide a nod: a generated face with no pulse nodding at 60 a minute, with a 1.5 px sway at 50 added, showed 60 in 0.15.0-test.5. Now the movement is judged at the rate being proven: the rate is the movement's when, on either axis, the movement there (its line and second harmonic) stands at least 3.9 dB above the rest of the head's movement with the strongest other rhythm taken out, and is at least 0.3% of the face's width (a heartbeat's own small shake of the head is not a nod). It is judged on a fixed 16 s grid. Once the check is handed the head, a second whose head is not seen across the window (rows starting or ending more than 1 s from its edges, a gap over 1 s, under 40 rows, or under 2.5 rows per cycle of the rate) is not evidence. The swayed nod now shows nothing; the nod alone shows nothing; a pulse at 70 under a nod at 90 still shows 70.

`headCentre` now also returns the face's width (`faceWidth`, cheekbone to cheekbone, in pixels), which `PulseCheck.push`'s `head` needs. `head: null` means the face mesh found no head that frame; leaving `head` out on every frame leaves movement unchecked, as before. `DemoRunner` hands over `null` for a frame with face regions but no landmarks, and `headCentre` gives `null` for a mesh of 454 points or fewer, so the rule needs MediaPipe's face mesh (468 points, or 478 with the irises): a frame source that never gives that mesh (no landmarks, or a 68-point face detector's) shows no heart rate while `headMotion` is on. New exports `headAtRate` (the movement at a rate on each axis, or why the window cannot be judged) and `headJudge` (`"carried"`, `"clear"` or `"blind"`), with the types `HeadPosition`, `HeadRow`, `HeadAtRate`, `HeadBlind` and `HeadJudgement`. `headLines` (the strongest-line statistic) is removed. `PulseCheckState.windowHead` reports how the latest second was judged.
